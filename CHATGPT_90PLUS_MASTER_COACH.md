### COURSE 1: Digital Systems & Architecture (`1101111`)

#### Core Mental Models (ELI5):
* **Binary/Hex:** Counting with 2 fingers vs. counting with 16 fingers.
* **De Morgan’s Laws:** Breaking a big roof (NOT bar) turns a multiplication into addition with little broken umbrellas.
* **Flip-Flop:** A seesaw toy that stays tipped up or down until someone pushes the other side.
* **Von Neumann:** A factory assembly line where the boss (CPU) reads an instruction card, grabs parts from the warehouse (RAM), and builds the product in the workshop (ALU).

#### High-Yield 5/5 Exam Targets:
1. **De Morgan's Theorems:**
   - Equation: $\overline{A \cdot B} = \overline{A} + \overline{B}$ and $\overline{A + B} = \overline{A} \cdot \overline{B}$
   - Truth tables (4 rows: 00, 01, 10, 11) + logic gate equivalences (NAND = Bubbled OR; NOR = Bubbled AND).
2. **Adders (Half Adder & Full Adder):**
   - Full Adder using two Half Adders and an OR gate.
   - Equations: $\text{Sum} = A \oplus B \oplus C_{in}$, $\text{Carry} = AB + BC_{in} + AC_{in}$.
   - Circuit schematic with logic gate symbols.
3. **Multiplexers (MUX 4:1 and 8:1):**
   - Concept: "Many inputs, one output train track switch controlled by select lines." Formula: $2^n = \text{inputs}$, $n = \text{select lines}$.
   - Function table + circuit diagram using AND-OR gates.
4. **Flip-Flops & Race Around Condition:**
   - SR vs JK vs D vs T flip-flops.
   - Race-around condition in JK ($J=1, K=1$ when pulse width $t_p > \Delta t$) and the Master-Slave solution.
5. **Von Neumann Architecture & Instruction Cycle:**
   - Diagram: CPU (PC, MAR, MDR, IR, AC, ALU) $\leftrightarrow$ System Bus $\leftrightarrow$ Memory.
   - 3-Phase Cycle: Fetch $\rightarrow$ Decode $\rightarrow$ Execute.

### COURSE 2: Fundamentals of Database Systems (`1101112`)

#### Core Mental Models (ELI5):
* **3-Tier Architecture:** 
  * *External Level:* The customer menu card at a restaurant.
  * *Conceptual Level:* The head chef's official recipe notebook.
  * *Internal Level:* The freezer storage boxes with barcode labels in the basement.
* **Normalization (1NF, 2NF, 3NF):** Marie Kondo tidying up a messy closet so every item has its own exact shelf and zero duplicates.
* **ACID Properties:** Ordering a burger on Zomato—money leaves your UPI only if the restaurant accepts the order (Atomicity); you don't end up with negative balance (Consistency); other orders don't steal your food (Isolation); and power cut won't delete your receipt (Durability).

#### High-Yield 5/5 Exam Targets:
1. **3-Tier ANSI/SPARC Architecture & Data Independence:**
   - Block diagram with External, Conceptual, and Internal levels.
   - Difference: **Logical Data Independence** (changing tables without breaking user views) vs. **Physical Data Independence** (changing hard disks/indexes without altering table schemas).
2. **ER Modeling (Entity-Relationship):**
   - Symbols: Rectangle (Entity), Diamond (Relationship), Oval (Attribute), Double Oval (Multivalued).
   - Cardinality: 1:1, 1:N, M:N with a clean Student-Course registration diagram.
3. **Normalization (1NF $\rightarrow$ 2NF $\rightarrow$ 3NF):**
   - **1NF:** Atomic values only (no comma-separated lists in a single cell).
   - **2NF:** 1NF + No Partial Dependency (every non-key column must depend on the *entire* Composite Primary Key).
   - **3NF:** 2NF + No Transitive Dependency (no $A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C$ indirect relationships).
   - Write out a messy 4-row Student-Course-Teacher table and show the decomposition step-by-step.
4. **Relational Algebra & SQL Operations:**
   - Selection ($\sigma$), Projection ($\pi$), Cartesian Product ($\times$), Natural Join ($\bowtie$).
   - DDL (CREATE, ALTER, DROP) vs. DML (SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE).
   - Inner Join vs. Left/Right Outer Join with Venn diagram sketches.
5. **ACID Properties & Transaction States:**
   - Bank transfer scenario: Account A debit ₹500 $\rightarrow$ Account B credit ₹500.
   - State diagram: Active $\rightarrow$ Partially Committed $\rightarrow$ Committed vs. Failed $\rightarrow$ Aborted.

#### Core Mental Models (ELI5):

  * *List `[ ]`:* An expandable notebook where you can cross things out and write again (Mutable, ordered).
  * *Tuple `( )`:* A stone tablet carved in stone that cannot be changed (Immutable).
  * *Set `{ }`:* A magic bag that instantly vaporizes duplicate toys (Unique, unordered).
  * *Dict `{k:v}`:* A contact book where you look up a person's name to instantly get their phone number (Key-Value, $O(1)$ fast).

#### High-Yield 5/5 Exam Targets:
1. **Prime Number Program with `for-else`:**
   - Explain why testing up to $\sqrt{n}$ is efficient.
   - Code snippet with proper indentation and commentary:
   ```python
   num = int(input("Enter number: "))
   if num > 1:
       for i in range(2, int(num ** 0.5) + 1):
           if num % i == 0:
               print(f"{num} is NOT prime")
               break
       else:
           print(f"{num} IS prime")
   else:
       print("Not prime")
   ```
2. **Comparison Matrix of Core Data Structures:**
   - 4-Column Table: Syntax, Mutability, Duplicates Allowed, Ordering, Indexing.
   - Common methods: `.append()`, `.pop()`, `.add()`, `.update()`, `.keys()`, `.values()`.
3. **List Comprehensions & Slicing:**
   - Syntax: `[expr for item in iterable if condition]`
   - Slice syntax: `seq[start:stop:step]` and reversing with `seq[::-1]`.
4. **Functions, `*args`, `**kwargs` & Lambda:**
   - Difference between positional arguments, arbitrary arguments (`*args` as tuple), and keyword arguments (`**kwargs` as dict).
   - Anonymous lambda functions: `square = lambda x: x * x`.
5. **File Handling & Exception Handling:**
   - Modes: `'r'`, `'w'`, `'a'`, `'r+'`.
   - The `with open(...) as f:` context manager.
   - `try-except-finally` block structure for handling `ZeroDivisionError` and `FileNotFoundError`.

#### High-Yield 5/5 Exam Targets:

2. **Filesystem Hierarchy Standard (FHS):**
   - Explain 6 core directories: `/`, `/bin`, `/sbin`, `/etc`, `/home`, `/var`, `/proc`, `/tmp`.
3. **File Permissions (Symbolic vs. Octal):**
   - 3 User classes: User (u), Group (g), Others (o).
   - 3 Permissions: Read ($r=4$), Write ($w=2$), Execute ($x=1$).

4. **Pipes (`|`), Redirection (`>`, `>>`, `<`), and Filters:**

   - Combining `grep`, `sort`, `uniq`, `wc`:

   - Process states: Running, Sleeping, Stopped, Zombie. Commands: `ps aux`, `top`, `kill -9 <PID>`.

### COURSE 5: Intellectual Property Rights (`1481311`)

#### Core Mental Models (ELI5):
* **Tangible vs. Intangible:** A bicycle is tangible (if someone takes it, you don't have it). A software algorithm or song is intangible (if someone copies it, you still have it, but they stole your value).
* **Patent vs. Copyright vs. Trademark:**
  * *Patent:* Protects the functional engine machine (How it works).
  * *Copyright:* Protects the written code or movie script (How it is expressed).
  * *Trademark:* Protects the brand logo and name (Nike swoosh, Apple logo).
  * *Trade Secret:* The secret Coca-Cola recipe locked in a vault with NDAs.

#### High-Yield 5/5 Exam Targets:
1. **3 Patentability Criteria (Indian Patent Act 1970):**
   - **Novelty:** Must be completely new worldwide (no prior art).
   - **Inventive Step (Non-obviousness):** Must not be obvious to a skilled craftsman.
   - **Industrial Applicability:** Must have real practical utility in industry.
   - Patent term: **20 years** from filing date.
2. **Copyrights & The Doctrine of Fair Dealing (Act 1957):**
   - What copyright protects: Literary, dramatic, musical, artistic works, and computer software source code.
   - Duration: **Author's lifetime + 60 years**.
   - **Fair Dealing Exemptions:** Private research, education/classroom teaching, criticism/review, and judicial proceedings.
3. **Trademarks & Trade Secrets:**
   - Types of trademarks: Wordmark, Logo, Shape, Sound mark. Term: **10 years** (renewable indefinitely).
   - Trade Secrets: Protected without registration via Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs) and non-compete clauses.
4. **Information Technology Act 2000 (Cyber Law):**
   - Section 43: Penalty for unauthorized access, data damage, virus introduction.
   - Section 66: Computer-related offences and hacking (punishment: up to 3 years imprisonment or ₹5 lakh fine).
